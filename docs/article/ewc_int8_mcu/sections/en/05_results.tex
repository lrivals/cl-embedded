\subsection{FP32 PC$\leftrightarrow$board parity (measured on-board)}

In FP32, the prediction-by-prediction parity is \emph{exact} in the frozen protocol (\num{1.000}) on both
datasets, and near-exact online (\num{0.9754} Pronostia, \num{0.9887} Monitoring) --- the online gaps stem
from the board's \texttt{float32} versus the PC's \texttt{float64} at decision boundaries.
Table~\ref{tab:parity} summarizes; Figure~\ref{fig:parity} illustrates.

\midrule

\paragraph{Latency and RAM (Gap~2/Gap~3).} All board latencies stay three orders of magnitude below budget:
inference \SI{50}{\micro\second} (Pronostia) / \SI{48}{\micro\second} (Monitoring); inference + online update
\SI{251}{\micro\second} / \SI{239}{\micro\second}, versus $\approx$\,\SIrange{33}{35}{\micro\second} for the
reference PC inference. The \texttt{.bss} footprint ranges over \SIrange{100}{145}{\kilo\byte}, below the
\SI{256}{\kilo\byte} available. Figure~\ref{fig:latency} places these values against the
\SI{100}{\milli\second} line.

\subsection{Collapse of naive PTQ (measured on-board)}

The \emph{legacy} INT8 quantization of the binary EWC head collapses on the board: F1 drops from
$\approx$\,\num{0.92} (FP32) to \num{0.138} (Pronostia) and \num{0.1337} (Monitoring), for an
INT8$\leftrightarrow$FP32 agreement of only $\approx$\,\numrange{0.60}{0.74}. Weight RAM is indeed divided by
4, but the metric is destroyed (Table~\ref{tab:int8board}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{INT8 \emph{legacy} vs FP32 on-board (\emph{measured}, \texttt{exp\_S36}). RAM $\div 4$ but collapsed
F1.}
\label{tab:int8board}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Dataset & F1 FP32 & F1 INT8 legacy & INT8/FP32 agr. & RAM $\div$ \\
\midrule
Pronostia  & \num{0.9164} & \num{0.138}  & \num{0.7364} & $\times 4$ \\
Monitoring & \num{0.9194} & \num{0.1337} & \num{0.5955} & $\times 4$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Diagnosis and recovery (bit-exact PC emulation)}

A \emph{bit-exact PC-emulated} ablation (exact kernel reference) isolates the cause and demonstrates recovery
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}). The dominant factor is the \emph{calibrated
per-tensor scale} on an \texttt{int32} accumulator: \texttt{per\_tensor\_calib} restores $+\,\num{0.88}$ F1
(Pronostia \num{0.9462}, Monitoring \num{0.9201}). The \texttt{q15} format exactly matches FP32.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{INT8 ablation ladder (F1, \emph{bit-exact PC emulation}, \texttt{exp\_S39\_ablation}).}
\label{tab:ablation}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Scheme & Pronostia F1 & Monitoring F1 \\
\midrule
FP32 (ref.)        & \num{0.9616} & \num{0.9194} \\
\texttt{legacy\_c} & \num{0.0663} & \num{0.1178} \\
\texttt{per\_tensor\_calib} & \textbf{\num{0.9462}} & \textbf{\num{0.9201}} \\
\texttt{per\_channel\_int8} & \num{0.9426} & \num{0.9187} \\
\texttt{q15}       & \num{0.9616} & \num{0.9194} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{RAM $\times$ F1 $\times$ latency trade-off}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} synthesizes the trade-off: the calibrated kernel brings the metric back to FP32 level
while keeping the RAM gain (INT8 $\div 4$, Q15 $\div 2$), with no prohibitive latency cost. The \emph{on real
hardware} recovery (Sprint~40, v2 kernel) is reported as "to be measured" for every cell not yet streamed, in
line with the "no invented number" rule.
